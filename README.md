# OBS Social 💬

**English** · [Português](#portugues)

A browser dashboard for putting your streams' comments on screen, right inside OBS — similar to vMix Social, but free and made for OBS Studio.

- Brings together the chats from **YouTube**, **Twitch**, **Kick** and **Bilibili** (experimental — turned on in 🧪 Labs) into a single feed, with **each person's photo** (or a colored initial, when there's no photo).
- You **click the comment** and it appears on the live screen, in its **network's color** (Twitch purple, YouTube red, Kick green, Bilibili blue).
- **YouTube Super Chats** show up with the real Super Chat color, both in the dashboard and on the live screen — and when they come in a foreign currency, they also show the **amount converted into the local currency** (reais, in this Brazilian example: `$5.00 ≈ R$ 25,00`). The converter covers **every currency in the world** (all the ISO codes and dozens of symbols: $, €, £, ¥, ₩, ₹, ₽, ₺, zł, Rp, RM, S/, ₦, ₲ and many others), understands number formats from various countries and uses a public exchange rate that updates on its own.
- **🔎 Global comment search**: a search box next to the tabs finds any comment you've lost track of — it searches by the **name of whoever wrote it** or by **words in the message**, without caring about capital letters or accents, and it covers all the tabs at the same time (Live, Queue, Super Chats, Members and Support). The search is **global**: besides what's on the screen, the program sweeps the **logs on disk** — the whole stream and every day that's been saved — so even with hundreds of pages the comment turns up. Clear the search and everything comes back.
- **Every button has a name**: hover over any icon and a label appears right away telling you what it does.
- **Adjustable interface size** (🔍 next to the theme): makes the whole dashboard and the text bigger or smaller proportionally, from 60% to 200% — your choice is saved.
- **🖱️ Arrange the screen by dragging**: on the **Screen and widgets** page of the settings (the 🖱️ button on the dashboard opens it directly), a map of your screen where you **drag each item** (featured comment, QR, giveaway, like meter, winstreak, audience and even **the overlay you uploaded** filling the whole screen) **anywhere you want** — it saves on its own and the overlay changes right away, even in the middle of the stream. There's a **grid** for positioning things precisely, in two modes: **fixed** (items stick to the grid lines) or **free** (the grid is just a guide); the cell size is adjustable (2.5%, 5% or 10%). **Click an item** to change its **size** with a slider, or put it back to the default with ↺. The 9 ready-made positions are still there as a shortcut in the settings, and the ◀ undoes one adjustment per click — a drag, a size, a layer, a piece — without having to reset everything.
- **👥 Real-time audience**: how many people are watching on each service appears in the chips at the top of the dashboard (updates every **10 seconds** — the shortest the platforms allow without blocking, and with no API keys), and an **audience widget for the public** can go on stream in two formats: **overall total with the logos** of the networks or **each network with its own audience**, always with the **network with the biggest audience in first place** (the order changes on its own during the stream) and with the **numbers on whichever side of the logos you choose** (right, left, above or below) — customizable like all the widgets (position, colors, size, background media and CSS). The chips also show the **⏱ stream time** for each network (how long it's been on the air, straight from the platform) and an **online monitor**: if the stream goes off the air, the chip dims with a 💤 — all of it updated every 10 seconds.
- **🕊️ Smooth message flow**: even on huge streams, comments come into the dashboard and the fixed chat at a steady pace — never in bursts. The ⏱️ on the dashboard picks the pace (real time, from 2 to 60 seconds, or 🖐️ manual), and the 🔃 button releases whatever is waiting right away (with a counter), always smoothly. The search and the logs get everything immediately, no matter the pace.
- Tabs side by side in the dashboard: **🔴 Live**, **📥 Queue** (comments waiting to go on stream), **💰 Super Chats**, **👑 Members** (YouTube) and **💝 Support** (donations).
- **🔍 Enlarged avatar**: right-click any avatar in the dashboard and the photo enlarges **in the dashboard and on the OBS screen** at the same time, with a **slider** for the size (it's remembered). With no photo, it enlarges the colored circle with the initial. It closes with the ✖, by clicking outside, or with Clear the screen.
- **🔢 Counters that count the whole stream**: the tabs and the columns show the real number for the day, with no cap, and each column has a third counter of **different people** who have already spoken. In the giveaway, the dashboard shows participants **and tickets in play**.
- **🔄 Reload the chats**: each column has a button that **restarts that network and fetches back the comments** the platform still has stored — that's how the hole left by a hiccup in the connection gets closed. You can leave it on automatic, from **"never" up to 60 minutes**.
- **⏳ Blinking your way**: the timer's **final stretch**, the **00:00** blink and the **Announcement** blink each have their own on/off switch, their own timing (how many seconds from the end it starts, and how long it keeps going) and their own **color**.
- **🎲 The giveaway die**: choose **how long it rolls** on the screen before the podium (0 = it goes straight there) and a **sound** for it — file, volume and where it plays — just like the timer alert.
- **🎬 The same animations everywhere**: entrance and exit offer the same 11 effects (appear, slide from the four sides, little hop, zoom, bounce, 3D flip, spin, blur) and the loop has 11 options, in the **featured comment**, in the **widgets** and in the **fixed live chat**.
- **📄 Name and text however you prefer**: the **fixed live chat** can show the text **below the name** and the **live dashboard** can show the text **next to the name** — each one with the other's option.
- **🔊 A sound alert that actually plays**: browsers only unlock audio after a tap on the page — OBS Social gets the sound ready on its own and, if it still ends up blocked, it shows a one-click invitation. In the **final 10 seconds** the timer turns **pulsing yellow** and the Announcement blinks along with it; when it hits zero, the red blink is slow and gentle. The timer shows the **tenths** on the live screen too.
- **🏆 Winstreak with celebrated milestones**: the record gets effects that **stay for as long as the number is on the milestone**, in the dashboard and on the live screen — a golden glow on the **tens**, a strong glow with **sparks** on the big milestones (50, 100, 200...) and colored sparks with a pulsing halo on the **doublings** (10, 20, 40, 80, 160...). The + and − buttons work for wins **and the record**, speed up when you hold them down, and the numbers can be typed in directly.
- **🏷️ Real badges, straight from the platforms**: 👑 OWNER, 🛡️ MOD, 💎 VIP, ⭐ SUB (with tier), 🎖️ MEMBER, 🏆 FOUNDER, 🏅 OG and ✔️ verified show up in the classic colors of each role — a short label on screen, the full name when you rest the mouse on it (including the sub tier and the member level). On top of that, OBS Social **fetches the original badges from YouTube, Twitch and Kick** (no login and no API key) and shows **each one's art next to the label**: the channel's drawing for the member levels, Twitch's own badges (Prime, Turbo, bits, gifts, events, channel-exclusive art) and Kick's — including the ones that don't have a fixed role, with the outline in the network's color. In **Settings → 🏷️ Chat badges** you can **turn each one of them off separately** (with a preview of how it looks on screen), remove just the images and keep the program's labels, or turn everything off at once — it applies to the live screen and to the dashboard at the same time.
- **🗑️ What moderation deletes disappears from the screen**: when a moderator deletes a comment, bans or mutes someone, or clears the chat, the comment disappears right away from the dashboard, from the fixed chat and from the featured comment on stream — the same way it disappears on the platform's site. It works on YouTube, on Twitch and on Kick, and it can be turned off in the settings.
- **🧱 Chats in columns**: on the Live tab, a switch shows the chat from **each network in its own column**, side by side, with a counter per network — and the optional **💬 + Unified** puts the shared chat's column alongside the others. The network filters choose which columns show up, the search goes back to unified mode while it's active, and you can **drag any column by its header (⠿)** into whatever order you want — the order is saved. Each network column has its **own smooth flow, independent of the others**: the messages trickle in at that column's pace (automatic by default), with the ⏳ showing its queue — a burst from YouTube never holds Twitch up. Each column also shows the **real total of messages from the stream** and has its **own pagination** (📄 from 5 to 100 per page, 20 by default) — no infinite scrolling. The 💬 Unified column uses the ⏳ to show how many messages are waiting for the smooth flow. The 🌊 also has a **"Per column"** mode: each column gets its own pace switch in the footer (right away, or 0.5s to 5s), independent of the others — and the **⏭️** button dumps the whole queue at once, so you can skip straight to the current messages.
- **🕘 Recover lost messages**: did your stream drop, or did the program stay closed? When it reconnects, OBS Social pulls whatever each service makes available from the recent chat — YouTube (the last few dozen the chat keeps), Twitch (through the community recent-messages service, the same one apps like Chatterino use), Kick (the recent ones from the site's API) and Bilibili (the last ~10) — **without duplicating anything** (repeated messages are recognized and ignored). You can turn it off in 🧪 Labs.
- **🧲 The dashboard your way**: in Settings → Dashboard, drag the tool icons and the tabs into whatever order you prefer (with a real-time preview) and choose whether the bar sits above or below the tabs. The dashboard also **adjusts to smaller screens**, and in 🖱️ Arrange the screen you can **pick any screen size** (Full HD, 2K, 4K, ultrawide, vertical or custom) to see how the overlays look.
- **Light/dark mode** (☀️/🌙 button) and settings organized under the gear button ⚙️ — each section (Connections, URLs, Featured comment, Fixed chat, Screen and widgets — with Arrange the screen, the widgets and Your overlays together —, Dashboard, Logs and 🧪 Labs) has its **own page**, with the item highlighted in the side menu.
- **📺 Horizontal and vertical overlay**: a switch chooses between the normal overlay, horizontal (16:9), vertical (9:16 — phone stream) or **the two together** — in the together mode, all the content (widgets, winstreak, colors, sizes) is **linked automatically** between the two screens, and only the **positions** are independent: 🖱️ Arrange the screen gets the 🖥️ Horizontal and 📱 Vertical tabs (the map stands upright), and the vertical stream uses its own URL.
- **Two URLs for OBS**: one for the **featured comment** (the one you click to show) and one for the **fixed live chat on the screen**, the way most streamers use it — with each person's photo, name and message.
- **An overlay you can customize in every way**: position, colors, fonts, sizes, shadows, the shape of the photos — and even your own CSS. With **several entrance AND exit animations** (slide in from any direction, gentle fade in/out, pop, zoom, bounce, 3D spin, twirl, blur...) for the featured comment, the fixed chat and each widget — you choose how each thing comes onto the screen and how it leaves. There are also **🔁 looping animations** to keep the overlays "alive" and grab attention: soft blink, glow, breathe, float, sway and jitter — the animation **goes away and comes back, over and over**, without stopping or for a fixed period that you set, with the cycle adjustable from 1 to 15 seconds. And **every animation has adjustable speed** (0 = the fast default; from 1 to 15 seconds for more cinematic entrances and exits). Finally, **everything can have screen time**: from 1 second to 60 minutes, with 0 = it stays until you take it away (this goes for the featured comment, the chat messages and each widget).
- **Your overlays**: upload any **image or video** (PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, MP4, WebM...) to use as the card's background or as a full-screen frame.
- **Live tools** in the bar above the tabs (icon buttons — hover your mouse over them to see what each one does):
  - **📱 QR Code**: type a name and a link and the QR shows up on screen right away, with its own 👁️/🙈 button to show/hide it (it is never removed by the other clear buttons). You can have **as many QRs as you want**: the **➕ "add more..."** button creates others, each with its own controls in **mini pages** inside the menu (◀ ▶ arrows to navigate, 🗑️ "remove this extra one") — they are all **saved indefinitely**, they all coexist on screen at the same time and are **100% independent**: each one can be dragged to its place in 🖱️ and appears in the widget editor (🧩) as "QR 2", "QR 3"... with **their own colors, size, animations and media**.
  - **🎁 Giveaway**: draws from among everyone who commented on the stream (one comment per person) and shows the podium on screen — 🥇 gold, 🥈 silver and 🥉 bronze, one below the other (or side by side, you choose). The **🎟️ tickets are configurable per level**: Twitch Prime, Tier 1, 2 and 3 (the tier comes from the chat's public sub badge), Kick sub and **founder** (each with its own weight) and YouTube members — including the **named levels** the channel creates (the name comes in the member badge; you set "Level name=tickets", one per line). And a **switch turns off the extra tickets** when the giveaway is open to everyone (1 ticket per person) — all with no login, reading only the public badges.
  - **📊 Like meter**: shows on screen how many people are **watching** the YouTube live stream vs. how many **likes** it has, and it updates on its own.
  - **🏆 Winstreak**: a win counter on screen, like `Solo: 1/34` — the record keeps up with your wins automatically (➕/➖ to mark, ↺ to reset). It also accepts **several at the same time** (one per game/mode): **➕ "add more..."** creates others, each with its own buttons in **mini pages** (◀ ▶ arrows, 🗑️ "remove this extra one"), all **saved indefinitely**, positionable one by one in 🖱️ and **100% independent** — each one appears in the widget editor (🧩) with its own colors, size and animations.
  - **📋 Clipboard**: a text field (up to 5000 characters) shared live among everyone who has the dashboard open — perfect for passing links and notes to a **co-host on the same network** (the dashboard shows the address they should open). The text stays saved even after restarting.
  - **✖ Clear screen**: takes all the overlays off the screen at once (except the QR code).

The first one is the **donations** one: a **generic donations URL** lets any automation tool send donations to the dashboard (💝 Support tab) and to the screen, with an amount and a color — turned off, the URL stops accepting them and the tab disappears from the dashboard.
- **📢 On-screen announcements**: write a message of up to 1000 characters (e.g.: "Back in 5 minutes!") and put it on the live screen, with **full formatting** — font, size, colors, alignment, bold/italic/underline, uppercase, shadow and all the entrance and exit animations.
- **🕐 Clock, stopwatch and timer**: a display in the **center of the tools bar** on the dashboard shows the **current time** (with the date, if you want) and opens three modes that can go to the screen — **clock** (with date), **stopwatch** and **timer**. The **time zone** is chosen in the settings (the computer's, GMT/UTC or any city in the world). The timer can **play a sound** when it hits zero — the program already comes with a ready-made alert, and you can swap it for your own (any format, up to 20 seconds, adjustable volume), choosing whether it plays **on the dashboard, on stream (the audience hears it) or both** and, if you want, it **takes the Announcement off the screen on its own** when the time runs out — with the Announcement **blinking gently in the final 10 seconds** so the audience notices. ## How to install (you only need to do this once)

1. **Install Node.js** (it's the engine that makes the program work):
   - Go to https://nodejs.org
   - Click the green **"LTS"** button and install it like any other program (next, next, finish).
2. **Download this project**:
   - Click the green **"Code"** button here on GitHub → **"Download ZIP"**.
   - **Tip (Windows):** before extracting, right-click the downloaded ZIP → **Properties** → check **"Unblock"** at the bottom → **OK**. That way all the files come out already cleared and Windows won't show the *"The publisher could not be verified"* warning when you open the program. (If you forget, no problem: you can sort it out later — see the questions at the end.)
   - Extract the ZIP into a folder that's easy to find (e.g. Documents).

## How to use (every time you go live)

1. Open the project folder and **double-click**:
   - **Windows:** `Iniciar (Windows).bat`
   - **Mac/Linux:** `iniciar-mac-linux.command`
   - A black window will open saying *"OBS Social is running!"* — **leave it open** during the stream.
2. Open your browser at **http://localhost:3000** → that's your **control dashboard**.
3. Click the **gear ⚙️** (top right corner) and connect the chats — the program **remembers the connections**: the fields come already filled in with what you used last time and, when you open the program, it **reconnects on its own** whatever was connected when you closed it (if you disconnect manually, it respects that and doesn't reconnect). And if one of the networks **freezes or breaks in the middle of the stream**, each card has a **🔄 button to restart the connection** — plus a button to **reconnect all of them at once** — without retyping anything:
   - **YouTube:** paste the link to your stream (or your channel's @) and click *Connect*.
   - **Twitch:** type the channel name (the one that shows up in `twitch.tv/name`).
   - **Kick:** type the channel name (the one that shows up in `kick.com/name`).
   - **Bilibili:** type the room number.
4. Set up OBS (only the first time). Under the dashboard's gear ⚙️, in the **"URLs for OBS"** card, there are two URLs with a **Copy** button:
   - **Featured comment** (`http://localhost:3000/overlay`): stays invisible until you click a comment.
   - **Fixed live chat** (`http://localhost:3000/chat`): shows the chat scrolling on the screen the whole time, with each person's picture — use it if you want to leave the chat fixed on screen, like most streamers do.
   - For each one you want to use: in OBS, under **Sources**, click the **+** → **Browser** (*Browser Source*), paste the URL, **Width** 1920 and **Height** 1080. You can use both at the same time, in separate sources.
5. During the stream: comments from every platform show up in the **🔴 Live** tab. **Click one** to send it straight to the screen (bottom center, by default), or **right-click** to open the menu: **📺 Send to the screen** or **📥 Send to the queue**.
   - A queued comment gets the **"📥 queued"** tag in the feed and waits in the **📥 Queue** tab (persisted to disk). There, right-clicking offers **send to the screen** or **remove from the queue**.
   - Every comment that **has already gone to the screen** gets the **"✓ read" tag with the time it was shown** (e.g. `✓ read 14:32`) and looks slightly dimmed in every tab (regular ones, Super Chats and Members) — that way you never show the same comment twice by accident. The tag survives restarts.
   - **💰 Super Chats**, YouTube **👑 Members** comments and **💝 donations** also show up separately, in their own tabs. Super Chats and member comments **don't go to the queue** — they only have the option of going straight to the screen.

💡 **Tip:** use the **"Send test comment"** button at the top of the dashboard to test how the overlay looks without being live.

## Security and access 🔐

### Why the browser says "not secure"

The warning shows up on **every** `http://` address and doesn't mean someone is looking at your dashboard.

- **`localhost`** is treated by browsers as a trusted origin by definition: the data **never leaves the computer**.
- **`192.168.x.x`** is your network's address: the data only travels between the machines connected to your router.
- The `https` padlock depends on a **certificate**, and a real certificate is only issued for a **public website name**. A home network address doesn't have one — and shouldn't have one.
- A self-signed certificate would be **worse**: a red danger screen on every device, every time, and the "solution" would be installing a trust file into the system — exactly the kind of thing that sets off the antivirus. That's why OBS Social doesn't do that.

What actually protects you: the server **only accepts connections from your own network**, with permissions by category and an optional password.

Everything is on the **🔗 URLs for OBS** page in the settings. The rules, in short:

- **This computer always has full access** — you're never asked for a password on the machine that runs the program.
- **Only your local network gets in.** OBS Social **has no remote access**: any connection coming from outside your network/Wi-Fi is refused, always, with no option to turn it on.
- **Restricted local network (default)**: whoever opens the dashboard over Wi-Fi only watches and uses **what you allow in the switches** — there's a sliding switch for each thing (clipboard, search, sending comments to the live screen, connections, tools, settings, media and logs). Or turn the restriction off to give **full freedom** to your mods.
- **With a password set**: whoever comes in over the network sees a password screen; with the right password, they get full access. The password is stored only as a scrambled code (salted hash) — never as text — and only the local computer can set, change or remove it.
- **🖥️ Machines connected right now**: the same page shows, live, how many machines are accessing the dashboard — with the machine's name (when the network reports it), device/browser and how many tabs each one has open. This list only appears on the streamer's computer.
- **Hidden URLs**: all the URLs and addresses in the settings stay hidden; click the eye 🙈/👁 to reveal each one.
- Repeated password attempts are locked out for a few minutes (brute-force protection).

## Feedback, support and updates 💜

- **📣 Feedback**: its own page in the settings — write your suggestion or problem and the button opens your email already filled in to send to the developer.
- **💖 Support the Developer**: if OBS Social helps you, you can give something back with any amount via QR code (NuBank, PicPay, LivePix and PixGG).
- **ℹ️ About**: version, credits for the creators and the **full changelog** in a menu that expands version by version.
- **🔄 1-click update**: on the About page, the "Check for a new version" button checks GitHub and, **only if you confirm**, installs the update by itself — without needing to download a ZIP. Your settings, password, logs and media stay intact; afterwards you just close and open the program. Nothing is updated without your confirmation. The program also **looks for an update by itself when it opens and once a day** and lets you know on the dashboard when there's a new version (you can turn this off; it never installs without your confirmation) — and, when it updates, it **closes and opens by itself**: the dashboard reloads and the overlay in OBS reconnects without you doing anything. There's also a ♻️ button in Connections to **restart the program** at any time.

## Frequently asked questions

**Windows shows "The publisher could not be verified" when I open `Iniciar (Windows).bat`. Is it dangerous?** No — it's the standard Windows warning for any file downloaded from the internet without a digital signature (there's no way to sign a `.bat` file; that only exists for `.exe` programs with a paid certificate). To fix it:
- **Quick:** uncheck the box **"Always ask before opening this file"** and click **Run** — the warning won't come back.
- **Permanent:** right-click the file → **Properties** → check **"Unblock"** at the bottom → **OK**. Tip: doing this on the **ZIP** before extracting means all the files come out already unblocked.
- **Bonus:** when you update using the button on the **About** tab, the program itself rewrites the files already unblocked — so, after the first update, this warning usually doesn't come back.

**Can I let someone else (a mod) control the dashboard?** You can, as long as they're on the same network/Wi-Fi: copy the "👥 Dashboard for other people" address on the 🔗 URLs for OBS page and choose what they can do (restricted or full freedom — better yet, set a password). Nobody outside your network can get in — OBS Social refuses connections from outside, always.

**My antivirus (Kaspersky, Defender...) flagged OBS Social. Is it a virus?** No — it's a behavioral false positive, common in programs that update themselves: OBS Social downloads the new version, swaps out its own files and reopens itself, and some antivirus programs think that pattern is "suspicious". The code is 100% open on GitHub for anyone to audit. To fix it: open the antivirus → Quarantine → **restore** the file (e.g.: `server.js`) → and add the **OBS Social folder to the antivirus's exceptions/exclusions**. In v0.9.2 the behavior that scared antivirus programs the most was removed.

**Do I have to pay anything?** No. Everything works with the platforms' public chat-reading methods.

**Does it work with Streamlabs / older OBS?** It works with any program that has a "Browser Source".

**Does the dashboard open on another computer/phone?** Yes — on the same Wi-Fi network, swap `localhost` for the IP of the computer that's running the program (e.g.: `http://192.168.0.10:3000`).

**Bilibili won't connect.** Bilibili sometimes blocks anonymous connections because of their own risk control. Wait a few minutes and try connecting again.

**Kick gave an error when connecting.** Kick uses protection (Cloudflare) that sometimes blocks the first attempt. Wait a few seconds and click *Connect* again.

**Port 3000 is already in use.** Start it on another port: `PORT=3500 node server.js` (Mac/Linux) or `set PORT=3500 && node server.js` (Windows).

## Como usar (toda vez que for fazer live)

1. Abra a pasta do projeto e dê **dois cliques** em:
   - **Windows:** `Iniciar (Windows).bat`
   - **Mac/Linux:** `iniciar-mac-linux.command`
   - Vai abrir uma janela preta escrita *"OBS Social está rodando!"* — **deixe ela aberta** durante a live.
2. Abra o navegador em **http://localhost:3000** → esse é o seu **painel de controle**.
3. Clique na **engrenagem ⚙️** (canto superior direito) e conecte os chats — o programa **lembra as conexões**: os campos já vêm preenchidos com o que você usou da última vez e, ao abrir o programa, ele **reconecta sozinho** o que estava ligado quando você fechou (se você desconectar manualmente, ele respeita e não reconecta). E se alguma rede **travar ou quebrar no meio da live**, cada cartão tem um botão **🔄 de reiniciar a conexão** — mais um botão para **reconectar todas de uma vez** — sem redigitar nada:
   - **YouTube:** cole o link da sua live (ou o @ do seu canal) e clique em *Conectar*.
   - **Twitch:** digite o nome do canal (o que aparece em `twitch.tv/nome`).
   - **Kick:** digite o nome do canal (o que aparece em `kick.com/nome`).
   - **Bilibili:** digite o número da sala.
4. Configure o OBS (só na primeira vez). Na engrenagem ⚙️ do painel, no cartão **"URLs para o OBS"**, há duas URLs com botão **Copiar**:
   - **Comentário em destaque** (`http://localhost:3000/overlay`): fica invisível até você clicar num comentário.
   - **Chat ao vivo fixo** (`http://localhost:3000/chat`): mostra o chat rolando na tela o tempo todo, com a foto de cada pessoa — use se quiser deixar o chat fixo, como a maioria dos streamers.
   - Para cada uma que quiser usar: no OBS, em **Fontes**, clique no **+** → **Navegador** (*Browser Source*), cole a URL, **Largura** 1920 e **Altura** 1080. Pode usar as duas ao mesmo tempo, em fontes separadas.
5. Durante a live: os comentários de todas as plataformas aparecem na aba **🔴 Ao vivo**. **Clique em um** para ele ir direto para a tela (no centro inferior, por padrão), ou clique com o **botão direito** para abrir o menu: **📺 Enviar para a tela** ou **📥 Enviar para a fila**.
   - Comentário na fila ganha a marcação **"📥 na fila"** no feed e fica aguardando na aba **📥 Fila** (persistida em disco). Lá, o botão direito oferece **enviar para a tela** ou **remover da fila**.
   - Todo comentário que **já foi para a tela** ganha a marcação **"✓ lido" com o horário em que foi mostrado** (ex.: `✓ lido 14:32`) e fica levemente apagado em todas as abas (comuns, Super Chats e Membros) — assim você nunca mostra o mesmo comentário duas vezes sem querer. A marcação sobrevive a reinícios.
   - Os **💰 Super Chats**, os comentários de **👑 Membros** do YouTube e as **💝 doações** também aparecem separados nas suas próprias abas. Super Chats e comentários de membros **não vão para a fila** — eles só têm a opção de ir direto para a tela.

💡 **Dica:** use o botão **"Enviar comentário de teste"** no topo do painel para testar o visual do overlay sem estar ao vivo.

## Personalizando o visual

Na **engrenagem ⚙️** você muda tudo em tempo real, separado por seção:

**🎨 Comentário em destaque** — posição na tela; cor do destaque (**a cor de cada rede social**, padrão, ou uma cor sua); cores de fundo e texto com transparência; fonte, tamanho, largura, cantos, espessura da barra colorida, espaço interno; formato da foto (redonda/quadrada); sombras no cartão e no texto; distintivos (com a arte da plataforma ou só os rótulos do programa); animação de entrada; esconder sozinho depois de X segundos.

**🎭 Perfis de overlay** — **moldes** completos do comentário em destaque (cores, letra, arte, posição e as peças) salvos com um nome: monte o visual, salve como perfil e troque o overlay inteiro em um clique. Com a **troca automática** ligada, cada comentário destacado veste o molde certo sozinho: um perfil para os comentários comuns e um por **faixa de valor** para Super Chats e 💝 doações — em qualquer moeda, convertida para reais pela cotação do dia. Cada faixa tem um botão 🧪 que põe no ar um Super Chat de teste com aquele valor, para conferir tudo antes da live. E o editor é **centrado no molde**: escolha um molde no seletor 🎭 "Editando" do 🖱️ Organizar a tela e tudo o que você mexer no destaque — posições, peças, cores, arte — grava naquele molde sozinho, com a prévia vestindo o molde ao vivo (cada molde também ganha uma URL fixa própria, `/overlay?perfil=NOME`). E desde a v0.58 o **Organizar a tela É o Editor visual de overlay**, ali mesmo na página Aparência: barra de perfis em cima, seletor de arte com miniatura, a tela como mesa de trabalho, a barra Selecionar elemento com a camada de cada elemento em setinhas ▲▼ e painéis em pixels para a seleção.

**🖼️ Suas artes** — moram junto dos 🎭 Perfis de overlay (uma seção só, sem interruptor experimental): envie qualquer imagem ou vídeo e escolha **onde cada mídia entra na tela** — 💬 fundo do cartão do destaque, 🖼️ moldura de tela inteira (crie a arte em 1920×1080 com fundo transparente — PNG para imagem, WebM para vídeo), 📱 QR, 🎁 sorteio, 📊 likômetro, 🏆 winstreak e 👥 audiência. A lista mostra em quais lugares cada arquivo está **EM USO**, as entradas 🏭 são as artes que vêm com o programa, e com um molde em edição no 🖱️ a arte escolhida para o cartão grava **dentro do perfil**. Dá para ajustar a opacidade da mídia.

**📺 Chat ao vivo fixo** — lado da tela (esquerda/direita), largura, estilo (balões com fundo ou só texto transparente), quantidade de mensagens, espaçamento, fonte e tamanho, cores (texto, fundo, nomes — por rede social, do próprio usuário ou uma cor única), formato das fotos, ícones, selos, barrinha colorida por rede, sombra no texto, Super Chats coloridos, animação, sumir sozinho depois de X segundos, e escolher **quais redes aparecem** nesse chat.

**🏷️ Distintivos do chat** — o liga/desliga de **cada distintivo separadamente** (dono, moderador, VIP, assinante, membro, fundador, OG, verificado e "os demais" que cada plataforma manda), com a **prévia de como cada um aparece na tela**; um interruptor para usar a **arte original da plataforma** ou só os rótulos coloridos do OBS Social; e a chave-mestra que desliga todos de uma vez. Tudo vale ao mesmo tempo para a live e para o painel. Nessa mesma página fica a opção de **sumir com os comentários apagados pela moderação**.

## Segurança e acesso 🔐

### Por que o navegador diz "não é seguro"

O aviso aparece em **todo** endereço `http://` e não significa que alguém esteja vendo o seu painel.

- **`localhost`** é tratado pelos navegadores como origem confiável por definição: os dados **não saem do computador**.
- **`192.168.x.x`** é o endereço da sua rede: os dados andam só entre as máquinas ligadas no seu roteador.
- O cadeado do `https` depende de um **certificado**, e certificado de verdade só é emitido para um **nome de site público**. Um endereço de rede caseira não tem — nem deveria ter.
- Um certificado autoassinado seria **pior**: tela vermelha de perigo em todo aparelho, toda vez, e a "solução" seria instalar um arquivo de confiança no sistema — exatamente o tipo de coisa que dispara o antivírus. Por isso o OBS Social não faz isso.

O que protege de fato: o servidor **só aceita conexões da própria rede**, com permissões por categoria e senha opcional.

Tudo fica na página **🔗 URLs para o OBS** das configurações. As regras, em resumo:

- **Este computador sempre tem acesso total** — nenhuma senha é pedida na máquina que roda o programa.
- **Só a sua rede local entra.** O OBS Social **não tem acesso remoto**: qualquer conexão vinda de fora da sua rede/Wi-Fi é recusada, sempre, sem opção para ligar.
- **Rede local restrita (padrão)**: quem abre o painel pelo Wi-Fi só assiste e usa **o que você liberar nos seletores** — há um seletor deslizante para cada coisa (área de transferência, busca, mandar comentários à tela, conexões, ferramentas, configurações, mídias e logs). Ou desligue a restrição para dar **liberdade total** aos seus mods.
- **Com senha definida**: quem entra pela rede vê uma tela de senha; com a senha certa, tem acesso total. A senha é guardada só como código embaralhado (hash com sal) — nunca em texto — e só o computador local pode definir, trocar ou remover.
- **🖥️ Máquinas conectadas agora**: a mesma página mostra, ao vivo, quantas máquinas estão acessando o painel — com nome da máquina (quando a rede informa), aparelho/navegador e quantas abas cada uma tem abertas. Essa lista só aparece no computador do streamer.
- **URLs escondidas**: todas as URLs e endereços das configurações ficam ocultos; clique no olho 🙈/👁 para revelar cada um.
- Tentativas repetidas de senha são travadas por alguns minutos (proteção contra força bruta).
- **Túnel ou proxy (ngrok, cloudflared, nginx...)?** Não recomendamos expor o painel assim — mas, se fizer, saiba que todo pedido chega ao programa como se viesse do próprio computador, e o computador local manda em tudo sem senha. Para esse caso, inicie com a variável de ambiente `OBS_SOCIAL_EXIGIR_SENHA_LOCAL=1`: o próprio computador passa a valer como rede (senha e modo restrito se aplicam também a ele).

## Perguntas frequentes

**O Windows mostra "O fornecedor não pôde ser verificado" ao abrir o `Iniciar (Windows).bat`. É perigoso?** Não — é o aviso padrão do Windows para qualquer arquivo baixado da internet sem assinatura digital (arquivos `.bat` não têm como ser assinados; isso só existe para programas `.exe` com certificado pago). Para resolver:
- **Rápido:** desmarque a caixinha **"Sempre perguntar antes de abrir este arquivo"** e clique em **Executar** — o aviso não volta mais.
- **Definitivo:** clique com o botão direito no arquivo → **Propriedades** → marque **"Desbloquear"** na parte de baixo → **OK**. Dica: fazendo isso no **ZIP** antes de extrair, todos os arquivos já saem liberados.
- **Bônus:** quando você atualiza pelo botão da aba **Sobre**, o próprio programa reescreve os arquivos já liberados — então, depois da primeira atualização, esse aviso costuma não voltar mais.

**Posso deixar outra pessoa (um mod) controlando o painel?** Pode, desde que esteja na mesma rede/Wi-Fi: copie o endereço "👥 Painel para outras pessoas" na página 🔗 URLs para o OBS e escolha o que ela pode fazer (restrito ou liberdade total — melhor ainda, defina uma senha). Quem está fora da sua rede não consegue entrar — o OBS Social recusa conexões de fora, sempre.

**Meu antivírus (Kaspersky, Defender...) acusou o OBS Social. É vírus?** Não — é um falso positivo de comportamento, comum em programas que se atualizam sozinhos: o OBS Social baixa a versão nova, troca os próprios arquivos e se reabre, e alguns antivírus acham esse padrão "suspeito". O código é 100% aberto no GitHub para qualquer pessoa auditar. Para resolver: abra o antivírus → Quarentena → **restaure** o arquivo (ex.: `server.js`) → e adicione a **pasta do OBS Social às exceções/exclusões** do antivírus. Na v0.9.2 o comportamento que mais assustava os antivírus foi removido.

**Preciso pagar algo?** Não. Tudo funciona com os métodos públicos de leitura de chat das plataformas.

**Funciona com Streamlabs / OBS antigo?** Funciona com qualquer programa que tenha "Fonte de Navegador" (Browser Source).

**O painel abre em outro computador/celular?** Sim — na mesma rede Wi-Fi, troque `localhost` pelo IP do computador que está rodando o programa (ex.: `http://192.168.0.10:3000`).

**A Bilibili não conecta.** A Bilibili às vezes bloqueia conexões anônimas por controle de risco deles. Espere alguns minutos e tente conectar de novo.

**O Kick deu erro ao conectar.** O Kick usa uma proteção (Cloudflare) que às vezes bloqueia a primeira tentativa. Espere alguns segundos e clique em *Conectar* de novo.

**A porta 3000 já está em uso.** Inicie com outra porta: `PORT=3500 node server.js` (Mac/Linux) ou `set PORT=3500 && node server.js` (Windows).
